FloraGrove · mark + wordmark, round 2

Five more pairings for the light/bold wordmark

Each mark is shown in a horizontal lockup, a stacked lockup (FLORA over Grove), as an app icon at 110, 58 and 29px, as a 16px favicon, and on cream. Scores are a design judgement from three lenses, not test results.

01 · Two weights

An outlined leaf and a solid leaf side by side: the light “Flora” and bold “Grove” of the wordmark, turned into a mark.

Best for: Wordmark-led brands; packaging, social avatars at 58px and up
Logo designer
The mark and wordmark share one idea, so the system explains itself.
Graphic designer
The outline gets thin below 29px; use the solid leaf alone as the favicon.
Art director
Calm and confident. Two leaves read as growing together, which is the grove story.

02 · Leaf-start G

The one-line G, now starting from a small leaf: Flora begins the stroke, Grove completes it.

04 · Seal

The one-line G inside a ring of type: “FloraGrove · Know why”. A secondary mark, not a replacement.

Best for: Secondary mark: stickers, merch, launch posts
Logo designer
Gives the brand a stamp for stickers, packaging and the App Store screenshots.
Graphic designer
Type is unreadable below 58px, so never use it as the app icon.
Art director
Adds craft and trust. Use it alongside a primary mark, not instead of one.

05 · fG monogram

A light sans “f” leaning into a bold serif “G”, taken straight from the wordmark.

Best for: Wordmark companion, favicon if bolded
Logo designer
Pure typography, so it matches the wordmark exactly.
Graphic designer
The light “f” thins out at 16px; the G carries it.
Art director
Classy but quiet. Typographic monograms rarely feel like a plant brand.
