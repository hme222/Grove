# FloraGrove personas: new plant owners, US and UK, iPhone

Date: 2026-10-24. **These are synthetic.** They are built from the 37-response survey in `docs/research/findings/2026-grove-concept-survey.md`, the confirmed audience order in `docs/product/confirmed-context.md`, and the app's own support for US and UK hardiness zones. They are a way to find predictable problems before real sessions. They are not evidence about real people, and no number below came from a real participant except where a survey figure is cited.

**Market assumption.** New plant owners (the confirmed MVP audience) in the US and UK, using an iPhone, mostly one-handed. If your market is narrower, tell me and I'll re-cut the set.

**What the survey says about this market:** 14 of 37 respondents were strict-new. New owners scored 2.06 of 5 on keeping a plant alive and 2.38 on judging light. Their top stressors were how much water (13 of 18), diagnosing yellow, brown or drooping leaves (13), and when to water (11). Pet safety worried 5 of 18. Reminders were the #1 dealbreaker feature, but 12 of 37 would delete the app over notification volume.

## Persona details

**1. Maya.** Plants: 6, all given or bought cheap. Anxious about overwatering. Opens the app mostly to check "do I need to do something." Frustrated by apps that shout. Environment: commute, evening couch, one thumb.

**2. Dev.** One fiddle-leaf fig, a dog that chews things. Opens the app at 7am for under a minute. Deletes anything that pings more than once a day. Frustrated by paywalls that appear after the first plant.

**3. Grace.** Reads with glasses and has her iPhone text size set larger. Wants plain words and clear labels. Frustrated by tiny grey text and unfamiliar terms. Doesn't trust that tapping will not "break something."

**4. Luis.** Reads English well but slowly, thinks first in Spanish, and idioms ("check-in time", "pip") do not map. Grows aloe, jade and cacti on a sunny patio. Frustrated by plants the app does not know.

**5. Priya.** Forgets, hyperfocuses, bounces off dense screens. Needs one thing to do, a visible result, and nothing competing. Frustrated by notifications and streak pressure she feels she's failing.

**6. Marcus.** Tremor makes small targets and precise taps hard. Uses the phone one-handed with a thumb. Frustrated by close-together buttons and swipe-only actions.

**7. Hannah.** Uses VoiceOver daily with the rotor on headings and landmarks. Frustrated by unlabeled buttons, custom controls that VoiceOver cannot reach, and animations that eat focus.

**8. Tom.** Gets flowers from his grandchildren and wants them to last. Not interested in "plant collections." Distrusts anything that says AI or asks for a card. Frustrated when an app assumes he owns plants.

**9. Aaliyah.** Dorm room with one small window, a shared Wi-Fi that drops, and no budget for apps. Frustrated by anything slow, anything behind a paywall, and anything that feels like a lecture.

**10. Sam.** Not the plants' owner, in a hurry, and one of the plants looks wrong. Outdoors or by a bright window, screen glare. Needs a quick, safe first check, and hates guessing.

## Where they overlap
- Grace, Luis and Hannah all benefit from plain, consistent names and text that scales.
- Priya and Dev both need less on screen and no unprompted pings.
- Marcus and Sam both need big, forgiving, one-handed targets.
- Maya, Sam and Tom all risk a harmful first move (overwatering) and need the safe check first.
- Aaliyah and a first-time user on a cold server share the same problem: waiting with no explanation.
